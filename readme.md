# Integrated Sensing and Synthesis: From Orthogonal Time-Frequency Space to Analog Frequency Division Multiplexing [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: It is anticipated that B5G and 6G wireless systems will adopt extremely high frequency (EHF) technology operating in the millimeter wave and THz bands, serving as a means to support applications such as the Internet of Things, edge computing, and smart cities, as well as scenarios involving vehicle-to-everything (V2X), high-speed rail, and non-ground wireless networks. These systems are typically subjected to heterogeneous and highly mobile conditions [3]. High mobility scenarios pose significant challenges to wireless communication systems due to the dual nature of the distributed wireless channels, also known as Time-Varying Multipath or Time-Frequency Selective [4]. This heterogeneous scattering environment causes deterioration in received signals through path delay and Doppler shift, leading to intersymbol interference (ISI) and Inter-Carrier Interference (ICI), significantly reducing communication performance under conventional and efficient modulation schemes (e.g., OFDM) [5].
In response to these challenges, there is an increasing expectation that B5G and 6G systems will provide ISAC capabilities, possibly with unified hardware and signal processing technologies [6]. In addition to providing environmentally aware and accurate/reliable positioning information for the aforementioned applications, the enhanced functionalities introduced by ISAC are crucial for improving spectrum and energy efficiency, as well as reducing the hardware costs of systems operating in high mobility scenarios [3].
Although it is difficult to predict which generation of the upcoming several/standards, namely B5G or 6G, will be adopted and implemented in commercial systems, this topic has been one of the most intensely discussed topics in recent years before the wireless system standardization forum. Notable examples include the 6G Intelligent Network and Services Association (6G-IA) where ISAC has been identified as a priority technology for its members, and the 5G Automotive Association (5GAA). ISAC is considered a supporting technology for cellular V2X services (please refer to https://5gaa.org/ and https: //6 g-ia.eu/ for more information on 5GAA and 6 G-IA respectively). While it can reasonably be expected that any form of actual deployment of 5G-based ISAC could leverage OFDM, specifically, cyclic prefix (CP) OFDM for downlink/sidelink and DFT-extended OFDM for uplink, for 6G, consideration should be given to new waveforms such as OTFS and AFDM to fully realize the potential of ISAC, as described in this article. In fact, important standardization bodies such as the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) and the Third Generation Partnership Project (3GPP), which are developing technical specifications for global mobile broadband systems, have recently included ISAC in their work plans and roadmaps. In November 2023, ETSI established a new group specifically responsible for ISAC.
In light of this trend, novel waveforms have recently been proposed due to their ability to maintain symbol orthogonality under bistatic scattering conditions. These are robust against high mobility and beneficial for ISAC because they inherently enable the estimation of environmental parameters such as the distance and velocity (i.e., delay and Doppler shift) of scatterers. One popular method is OTFS signaling [7], which utilizes an inverse Singular Finite Fourier Transform (ISFFT) to modulate a 2D grid directly in the delay-Doppler domain, gaining significant attention for B5G systems with superior performance compared to current waveforms such as OFDM [8].
Indeed, it is easy to show that the full delay-Doppler representation of the channel in OTFS inherently conveys information about the speed and range of scatterers in the form of corresponding multipath delays and Doppler shifts, thereby indicating significant benefits for ISAC. Consequently, numerous ISAC techniques based on OTFS have been proposed to directly extract interpretable path delay and Doppler parameters from channel state information (CSI), which has been proven to compete with OFDM and Frequency Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW) radar in terms of detection performance and offers higher robustness against mobility and achievable capacity [9].
An alternative strategy for designing ISAC-friendly and mobile-resilient waveforms is adopting chirp-based multicarrier methods [10]. Although linear frequency modulation domain designs are attractive due to their inherent spread spectrum characteristics and potential for full duplex operation, an important and common shortcoming of these early approaches is their lack of adaptability to channel delay and Doppler spread, a result of nonparameterizable transformations in their design.
The recent ideas to alleviate the next drawback are AFDM waveforms [11] that utilize Discrete Affine Fourier Transform Inverse (IDAFT) [12] to modulate information symbols into distorted time-frequency domain, thus generating desired delay-Doppler orthogonality while maintaining necessary flexibility. The configurable parameters of AFDM also come with other desirable attributes such as full diversity guarantee and increased throughput [11], making AFDM a strong candidate for ISAC enabling waveforms in B5G and 6G systems.
This paper aims to comprehensively analyze the basic principles and future of ISAC technology in heterogeneous high mobility scenarios, comparing prominent candidate waveforms in the form of OTFS and AFDM, with a focus on OTFs and AFDM. The analysis shows that the novel delay Doppler orthogonal design of OTFS and AFDM is beneficial for signal processing in communication and sensing functions, advocating for their integration. These insights may hold significant interest and value not only for academic circles but also for standardization engineers involved in the development of future generations of mobile broadband systems across various vertical industries.
